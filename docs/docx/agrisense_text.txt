CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION

Background of Study

The global food system is under increasing strain as post-harvest inefficiencies continue to undermine food availability and farmer livelihoods. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization, approximately one-third of all food produced globally is lost each year due to spoilage [1], with staple grains such as rice and corn being particularly vulnerable during storage. Collectively, these pressures underscore that addressing post-harvest grain spoilage through affordable, proactive, and technology-driven solutions is both a local necessity and a global development imperative. These losses carry direct implications for three United Nations Sustainable Development Goals: SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), which advocates for sustainable and resilient food production systems; SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure), which promotes inclusive industrialization and technological innovation; and SDG 13 (Climate Action), which acknowledges that rising temperatures and humidity — especially in tropical regions — accelerate the conditions that lead to grain deterioration [2]. 
Globally, post-harvest grain losses represent a systemic challenge that disproportionately affects smallholder farmers who lack access to adequate storage technologies and real-time monitoring tools. In the Philippines specifically, the Philippine Statistics Authority has documented substantial annual post-harvest losses in rice and corn, consistently identifying storage spoilage as one of the primary contributing factors [3]. The country's tropical climate — marked by persistently high temperatures and relative humidity — creates inherently hostile conditions for grain preservation, compounding the vulnerability of poorly equipped storage facilities. In Davao region, due to its tropical climate marked by high temperatures and humidity, it accelerates grain deterioration, yet most local storage within the region still rely on reactive, manual inspections that fail to prevent spoilage [4]. The prohibitive cost of commercial-grade monitoring systems further widens the technological gap between large commercial grain operators and subsistence-level farming households [5].
The core problem identified in this study is the inadequacy of current grain storage monitoring practices among smallholder farmers in Davao region, whose reliance on manual inspection leaves stored harvests chronically exposed to moisture migration, condensation, mold growth, and pest infestation. The root causes include the absence of real-time environmental sensing infrastructure, limited technical capacity among local farmers, and the financial inaccessibility of commercial monitoring solutions [5]. These conditions produce cascading effects: significant income losses for farming households, reduced grain quality and market value, and broader threats to community food security. Framed through an objective tree lens, the desired outcome is a shift from reactive and delayed responses to proactive, automated, and continuous monitoring — enabling grain storage operators to detect and respond to dangerous environmental thresholds before spoilage occurs, thereby protecting both harvest quality and farmer income. 
	Existing grain monitoring solutions, where available, remain largely inaccessible to smallholder farmers due to their high cost and technical complexity, leaving a critical gap between commercially available technologies and the operational realities of small-scale grain storage in tropical agricultural communities. This study addresses that gap by developing AGRISENSE, a Smart IoT-Based Grain Quality Preservation Monitoring System that integrates environmental sensors to continuously measure temperature, humidity, and dew point inside grain warehouses, and automatically triggers aeration mechanisms when storage conditions approach unsafe levels [5]. Conducted under the research, development, and extension mandate of Davao del Norte State College as provided under Republic Act 7879 [6], this study aims to deliver an affordable, user-friendly, and technically reliable solution tailored to the needs of smallholder farmers.


Objectives of the Study
The main objective of this project is to design and develop a Smart IoT-Based Grain Quality Preservation Monitoring System that accepts real-time temperature and humidity sensor data as input from grain storage warehouses and produces automated ventilation control signals, real-time forecasting displays, and alert notifications to help prevent spoilage of stored grains. Specifically, the project aims to:
Develop a multi-sensor environmental monitoring system for grain storage warehouses, processes them through sensors measuring temperature (°C) and relative humidity (%) at intervals, and produces real-time sensor data transmitted wirelessly to the central IoT controller (ESP32) for evaluation and storage.
Implement an automated ventilation control system that sends control signals to exhaust fans or any ventilation system available based on humidity threshold comparisons to maintain optimal storage conditions and prevent buildup of moisture.
Develop a realtime application thru webpage or mobile app to visualize sensor data and ventilation status, providing graphical monitoring and automated alerts when storage thresholds are exceeded.
Implement a data logging and analytics system that processes 30 day minimum historical sensor data using statistical algorithms to produce trend reports monthly, quarterly and annually, with downloadable CSV files.

Scope and Limitation
	This project focuses on the design and development of grain bins and silos in Davao region. The system encompasses four primary operational processes: continuous environmental data collection, real-time data processing and dew point computation, automated ventilation control, and web and mobile-based monitoring with alert notification. Development and testing of the prototype will be conducted within one grain bin storage warehouse in the study locale, and while the system is designed to be applicable to grain bins and silos, findings and system configurations may require adaptation before deployment in other grain storage contexts or geographic locations.
The system is built around an ESP32 microcontroller integrated with temperature and humidity sensors that continuously measure ambient environmental conditions inside enclosed grain storage facilities in real time. The microcontroller processes raw sensor data to compute the dew point — the critical threshold indicator for dangerous moisture condensation that leads to mold growth, pest infestation, and grain deterioration — and continuously compares live readings against predefined safe storage thresholds. When monitored values exceed these thresholds, the system automatically activates exhaust fans to regulate the internal storage environment without requiring manual intervention. All processed sensor data is simultaneously transmitted to a mobile application interface that displays live readings, generates automated alerts when unsafe conditions are detected, and logs all environmental data for historical record-keeping and post-harvest decision-making.
However, AGRISENSE is limited to monitoring the ambient air conditions inside grain storage facilities. System performance is dependent on the consistent operation of the ESP32 microcontroller and the accuracy of environmental sensors under actual tropical warehouse conditions, with sensor readings potentially affected by physical obstructions, facility size, irregular power supply, or suboptimal sensor placement. The mobile application requires a stable internet or local network connection to function effectively as a prototype.

Definition of Terms

Aeration: The process of moving ambient air through the grain mass to manage grain temperature and moisture. In this study, it refers to the automated activation of exhaust fans or blowers by the AGRISENSE system.
AGRISENSE: The name of the proposed system, representing a Smart IoT-Based Grain Quality Preservation and Monitoring System designed to automate storage environment management.
Ambient Air Conditions: The atmospheric conditions (specifically temperature and relative humidity) existing within the enclosed headspace of the grain storage facility.
Automated Ventilation Control: A closed-loop mechanism within the system that triggers physical relays to turn fans on or off based on whether real-time sensor data exceeds predefined safety thresholds.
Data Logging: The process of capturing and storing time-stamped environmental data over a minimum period of 30 days to facilitate trend analysis and historical reporting.
Dew Point: The specific temperature at which the air becomes saturated and water vapor begins to condense into liquid. This study uses computed dew point as a primary indicator to prevent moisture-related spoilage.
ESP32 Microcontroller: The low-cost, low-power system-on-a-chip with integrated Wi-Fi and dual-mode Bluetooth used as the central processing unit for the AGRISENSE prototype.
Grain Spoilage: The deterioration of grain quality caused by mold growth, pest infestation, or moisture migration, leading to reduced market value and potential health risks (e.g., aflatoxins).
Internet of Things (IoT): A network of physical objects (sensors and actuators) that use internet connectivity to exchange data. In this study, it enables remote monitoring and cloud-based alerting.
Post-Harvest Losses: The measurable decrease in grain quantity or quality occurring between the time of harvest and the moment of consumption or sale.
Proactive Monitoring: A management strategy that utilizes real-time data to anticipate and correct hazardous storage conditions before physical spoilage occurs, as opposed to reactive manual inspections.
Relative Humidity (RH): The amount of water vapor present in the air, expressed as a percentage of the amount needed for saturation at the same temperature.
Relay Signal: An electrical signal sent by the ESP32 to a switch (relay) that controls high-voltage equipment, such as industrial exhaust fans.
Smallholder Farmer: For the purpose of this study, farmers in the Davao Region who manage small-scale agricultural operations and typically lack access to expensive industrial-grade storage technologies.
Threshold: A predefined numerical value for temperature or humidity which, when approached or exceeded, triggers an automated system response or user alert.

SYSTEM ANALYSIS
System Architecture
The system architecture of the project AgriSense: Smart IoT Monitoring and Forecasting System for Grain Spoilage, as shown in Figure X, presents a structured framework that illustrates how the different components of the system interact to support real-time monitoring, predictive forecasting, and automated intervention. The architecture is composed of four main layers: hardware (perception layer), data management (network and storage), intelligence (analytical engine), and software (user interface).
At the hardware level, the system utilizes IoT-based modules equipped with temperature, humidity, and moisture sensors installed within the grain bins. These sensors continuously collect environmental data and transmit it through secure communication protocols to the central system. This enables real-time data acquisition and ensures that any conditions leading to grain spoilage are immediately captured and recorded.
At the intelligence level, the system includes a centralized analytical engine that processes incoming sensor data using predefined safety thresholds and predictive modeling techniques. The system analyzes the data to identify immediate risks and generates a 7-day outlook to forecast potential spoilage timelines. When abnormal conditions or high-risk trends are detected, the system automatically triggers alerts and sends control commands to the physical ventilation actuators.
The data management component consists of a relational database and cloud storage, where all sensor logs, forecast results, and user account records are securely stored. This enables efficient data retrieval for historical trend analysis and long-term reporting. At the software level, a web-based dashboard is provided, allowing warehouse staff and administrators to monitor real-time data, view visualized forecast models, and manage system configurations effectively.

Technologies, Concepts, and Theories
This section presents the key models, algorithms, and emerging technologies utilized in the development of AgriSense: Smart IoT Monitoring and Forecasting System for Grain Spoilage. It explains how data is processed from acquisition to decision-making, as well as the tools and technologies used to implement the system. The discussion follows the major stages of the system workflow, including data collection, pre-processing, feature extraction, and forecasting.
Data Collection
Data collection is performed through IoT-based sensor modules installed within grain silos, including temperature, humidity, and moisture sensors. These devices continuously capture environmental data in real time and transmit it to the central system via wireless communication protocols. This process ensures that the system receives accurate and timely data necessary for monitoring the health of stored grain and detecting potential spoilage risks.
Data Pre-Processing
Before analysis, the collected data undergoes pre-processing to improve its quality and reliability. This includes filtering sensor noise, handling missing or inconsistent values caused by network fluctuations, and normalizing readings to a standard format. Pre-processing ensures that the input data is clean and suitable for further analysis, reducing the likelihood of incorrect risk indices or false notifications.
Feature Extraction
Feature extraction involves identifying relevant attributes from the collected data that contribute to spoilage detection and forecasting. Key features include ambient temperature levels, moisture content percentages, and the rate of humidity change over time. These features represent the current internal environment of the silo and serve as inputs to the forecasting algorithm. Extracting meaningful features improves the accuracy and efficiency of the system's predictive capabilities.
Forecasting
The system utilizes a combination of threshold-based logic and time-series analysis to classify environmental conditions as safe, warning, or danger zones. Threshold-based methods trigger immediate ventilation responses when sensor values exceed predefined limits, while predictive techniques analyze historical patterns to generate a 7-Day Spoilage Forecast. This hybrid approach enhances the system's ability to not only respond to current breaches but also anticipate future risks before grain quality is compromised.

Problem Tree and Objective Tree Discussion
The Problem Tree Diagram, systematically maps the causes, core problem, and cascading effects related to the spoilage and quality degradation of stored rice and corn grains in Davao Region. The core problem at the center of the diagram is driven by four root causes: persistently high humidity and temperature inside grain storage facilities accelerated by the Philippines' tropical climate; the absence of real-time monitoring that allows dangerous environmental changes to go undetected until spoilage is already irreversible; delayed detection of spoilage indicators resulting from reliance on infrequent manual physical inspection; and inadequate ventilation and storage management that allows heat and moisture to accumulate unchecked inside warehouses. These root causes collectively produce four direct effects — grain prone to spoilage and reduced market value, loss of income and revenue for farmers and storage operators, reputation damage for grain traders and cooperatives, and increased post-harvest losses across the agricultural community — which further escalate into two higher-order consequences: severe financial strain on farming households and an ultimate threat to the long-term viability of smallholder grain farming businesses in the municipality.
The Objective Tree Diagram, directly mirrors the Problem Tree by converting each identified problem into a corresponding positive outcome or solution, organized around the central objective of developing a system that monitors and controls the humidity and temperature of a grain storage facility. Each of the four root causes is addressed by a corresponding system-level solution: controlled humidity and temperature management through automated environmental sensing and ventilation control; continuous IoT-based environmental monitoring that eliminates reliance on manual inspection; fast detection of spoilage indicators through real-time dew point computation and automated alert notifications; and adequate ventilation and storage management through the automated activation of exhaust fans or aeration blowers in direct response to sensor-detected threshold breaches. These four solutions produce four direct positive outcomes — grain safety from spoilage and degradation, high income and revenue for farmers and storage operators, a strengthened reputation for grain traders and cooperatives, and increased post-harvest income for farming households — which collectively contribute to two long-term benefits at the top of the diagram: increased gross profit and the strengthened overall viability of agricultural businesses, enabling smallholder grain farmers in Davao region to sustain, grow, and remain competitive within the grain agriculture industry.
